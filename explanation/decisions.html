Decisions ​

ADR-lite — the decisions that shape hypha, the alternatives rejected, and the consequence each carries. Full history lives in the org ledger (project/adrs/ — ADR-0091 is the entity-plane contract; PL-005 is the extraction plan).

Substrate over sessions (ADR-0091) ​

Decision: the distributed layer is shaped around generic Entity {id, kind, indexDoc, health} values and namespaced ops — never around sessions or campaigns.

Alternatives: keep the entity plane inside peregrin shaped around sepia-session (every new workload kind — CI jobs, sturn claims, cicada schedules — would fork the substrate); a workload-specific plane per integration (N sync/arbitration implementations to keep correct).

Consequence: integrations plug in by implementing the entity contract in their own repo; the substrate stays kind-agnostic — no sepia/campaign/session types in these crates, enforced by review.

Snapshot bytes are opaque end-to-end ​

Decision: Snapshot {entity, ref, bytes} — serialize on the owner, import on the target; hypha never parses the payload.

Alternatives: a substrate-owned snapshot schema (every integration's state model becomes hypha's problem); references to external stores (transfer breaks when the store is unreachable or versioned differently).

Consequence: migration semantics (campaign-snapshot/v1, sepia session IR) belong entirely to the integration — hypha guarantees transport, ordering, and arbitration, nothing more.

Ops are namespaced and TTL'd ​

Decision: op kind is <integration>.<verb> with entity.* reserved for the substrate; outbox entries carry idempotency keys and TTLs, and Turn-class ops dead-letter on first failure.

Alternatives: a flat global verb namespace (collisions across integrations); infinite retries (a poisoned op blocks its entity's FIFO forever).

Consequence: the substrate can route and dedupe without understanding any verb; a failing op surfaces as dead-letter instead of silent backlog.

Policy is injected, not owned ​

Decision: hypha-control is a pure arbitration library — lock registry, takeover, pick_owner — with no I/O; the hub boots an empty KindPolicies and integrations supply policy.

Alternatives: policy baked into the hub (who-may-act rules become substrate concerns); policy in each node (split-brain arbitration).

Consequence: arbitration is testable pure code; orchestration policy stays in peregrin where it belongs — the substrate decides mechanism, never intent.

Extracted from peregrin, composed upward ​

Decision: hypha is the entity plane lifted out of peregrin (PL-005); peregrin-node becomes a thin composition root that registers entity planes on a hypha node host.

Alternatives: keep the plane inside peregrin (the substrate could never serve non-orchestration workloads); a ground-up rewrite (the wire shapes and outbox semantics were already proven in production inside peregrin).

Consequence: dependency direction is one-way — peregrin → hypha → ports. Orchestration sits on the substrate; the substrate never imports orchestration.
